🌎 Geopolitics & World Affairs
US Begins Hormuz Minesweeping as Fragile Ceasefire Teeters

President Trump announced Saturday that the US has begun clearing mines from the Strait of Hormuz, declaring that Iranian minelaying vessels have all been sunk. Iranian media, however, reported that one vessel was merely turned back after receiving a warning shot — a telling gap in the official narratives. Meanwhile, Reuters reports that Americans are voicing growing anxiety about the conflict after five weeks of war that has jolted energy markets and threatened global economic stability.

Why it matters: Hormuz carries roughly 20% of global oil traffic; if minesweeping succeeds and the two-week ceasefire holds, energy markets could stabilize significantly — but a single miscalculation could blow the whole deal apart.

Israel Strikes South Lebanon; Iran Calls Peace Talks "Unreasonable"

Lebanon's government said 10 people were killed Saturday in Israeli strikes on the south of the country, even as the US–Iran ceasefire nominally remained in effect. The Times of Israel notes that Iran's senior delegation, including Parliament Speaker Ghalibaf and Foreign Minister Araghchi, flew into Pakistan — the country brokering talks — on Saturday, yet Tehran has publicly labeled the peace terms "unreasonable."

Why it matters: Continued Israeli operations in Lebanon risk drawing Hezbollah back into full confrontation and could give Iran political cover to walk away from ceasefire negotiations entirely.

Xi Warns Taiwan's Opposition: Independence Is Non-Negotiable

Chinese President Xi Jinping delivered a blunt message to Taiwan's opposition leaders, telling them Beijing will not tolerate any move toward formal independence. The remarks, reported by Reuters, came as Xi received opposition delegates in what Beijing framed as a cross-strait dialogue — though Taiwan's ruling party was pointedly not invited.

Why it matters: With US military attention stretched between the Iran conflict and Pacific commitments, Beijing appears to be seizing the moment to pressure Taiwan's political class and test Washington's resolve.

 
🚀 Science & Technology
Artemis II Crew Splashes Down Safely, Closing a Historic Chapter

The four-person Artemis II crew successfully splashed down in the Pacific Ocean on Saturday, CBS News reports, completing NASA's first crewed lunar flyby mission since Apollo 17 in 1972. The mission, which included a breathtaking "Earthset" photograph that drew comparisons to Apollo 8's iconic imagery, sets the stage for Artemis III — the planned crewed lunar landing.

Why it matters: A successful splashdown validates the Orion capsule's deep-space life support systems and clears the final technical hurdle before humans set foot on the Moon for the first time in over 50 years.

Tesla's Supervised Self-Driving Gets Its First European Green Light — in the Netherlands

Dutch regulators approved Tesla's supervised Full Self-Driving software for use on public roads, making the Netherlands the first European country to do so, according to Reuters via The Jakarta Post. The approval covers supervised operation only — a human must remain ready to intervene — but it opens the door to broader EU regulatory conversations that Tesla has been pursuing for years.

Why it matters: Europe's 450-million-person market has been largely closed to advanced driver-assistance features; a Dutch precedent could accelerate approvals across the EU and reshape the competitive landscape for autonomous vehicle makers.

 
💼 Business & Finance
Nexstar–Tegna Merger Blocked Again as Media Consolidation Battle Drags On

A federal judge extended a restraining order on the proposed $6.2 billion merger between local TV giants Nexstar and Tegna, AP News reports, keeping the deal in legal limbo. The ruling prolongs uncertainty for thousands of local TV journalists and staff across the two companies' combined 200-plus stations, as regulators and courts continue to scrutinize the deal's impact on local news competition.

Why it matters: If the merger ultimately fails, it signals a tougher regulatory environment for legacy media consolidation — and leaves both companies to navigate a brutal local TV ad market independently.

Gold Miners Shine as Ceasefire Anxiety Keeps Safe-Haven Demand Elevated

Gold miner stocks and related ETFs have delivered outsized returns as the US–Iran conflict and ceasefire uncertainty push investors toward safe-haven assets, Investor's Business Daily reports. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq extended their win streaks Friday amid cautious optimism about the ceasefire, though analysts warn that any breakdown in Hormuz negotiations could send oil surging and equities retreating sharply.

Why it matters: The divergence between resilient equity markets and booming gold signals that investors are simultaneously betting on a ceasefire resolution and hedging aggressively against the possibility it collapses.
