🌎 Geopolitics & World Affairs
U.S.–Iran Talks Limp Into Sunday With Hormuz in the Crosshairs

Iran confirmed negotiations with the United States will continue Sunday even as both sides acknowledge "serious disagreements" remain unresolved, with Tehran insisting its red lines include retaining authority over the Strait of Hormuz and a ceasefire in Lebanon. Meanwhile, Trump claimed U.S. forces have begun minesweeping operations in the strait after declaring all Iranian minelaying ships have sunk — a claim Iranian media partially disputed, saying one vessel was merely turned back after warnings. The IRGC threatened to "deal severely" with any warships entering the zone. (The Times of Israel)

Why it matters: Hormuz carries roughly 20% of global oil traffic; a breakdown in talks this weekend could reignite the oil price spike that rattled markets earlier in the week and push the fragile two-week ceasefire toward collapse.

London Arrests 200+ at Palestine Action Protest in Trafalgar Square

Metropolitan Police arrested more than 200 demonstrators in central London's Trafalgar Square on Saturday at a rally held in defiance of the government's ban on Palestine Action, a direct-action group. Officers moved in on crowds holding placards and removed protesters physically, in scenes broadcast widely across social media. The arrests mark one of the largest single-event protest crackdowns in the UK in recent memory. (AP News)

Why it matters: The mass arrests will intensify the civil-liberties debate in Britain over whether banning activist groups and policing dissent crosses a constitutional line, with legal challenges almost certain to follow.

Artemis II Splashes Down — Now What?

The Artemis II crew completed a successful splashdown in the Pacific Ocean on Saturday, bringing to a close the first crewed lunar flyby mission in more than 50 years. NASA and CBS News coverage confirmed all four astronauts are safe, and analysts are already turning their attention to what comes next for deep-space human exploration. The mission looped around the moon before returning home, validating the Orion capsule's life-support and navigation systems for longer future voyages. (CBS News)

Why it matters: A clean splashdown removes a major technical risk factor and clears the path for Artemis III — the mission intended to put boots back on the lunar surface — which now faces political and funding scrutiny as its primary hurdle.

 
💼 Business & Finance
OpenAI Memo Leaked: Anthropic Is the Enemy Within the AI Arms Race

A leaked internal memo reveals that OpenAI's leadership now views Anthropic — not Google or Meta — as its single most dangerous competitive threat, a remarkable shift given Anthropic was founded largely by OpenAI alumni. The document surfaces just days after Anthropic's Claude Mythos security project made headlines for uncovering thousands of zero-day vulnerabilities, and as CoreWeave locked in a multi-year infrastructure deal with Anthropic. (TipRanks)

Why it matters: When the dominant player in a technology sector identifies a specific upstart as an existential threat, capital flows and partnership dynamics across the entire industry tend to realign quickly — expect accelerated model releases and pricing battles from both camps.

Tesla Heads for an Eighth Straight Weekly Loss as Investors Wait for a Catalyst

Tesla closed out last week on track for its eighth consecutive weekly decline, a losing streak that underscores deepening investor anxiety over slowing EV demand, brand damage tied to CEO Elon Musk's political activities, and intensifying competition from Chinese automakers. Analysts note the stock is awaiting a concrete positive catalyst — whether a new model reveal, a robotaxi milestone, or a resolution to the trade-war tariff uncertainty that has clouded manufacturing cost projections. (TipRanks)

Why it matters: Eight straight down weeks for the world's most-watched EV stock reflects broader sentiment about the sector's near-term profitability, and prolonged weakness could trigger forced selling by leveraged retail holders who piled in during the 2024 rally.

S&P 500 Rebounds 3.6% Last Week but Remains in the Red for 2026

The S&P 500 gained 3.6% last week to close at 6,816.89, recovering meaningfully from recent lows but still sitting 0.4% below where it started the year and 2.3% off its January 27 all-time closing high of 6,978.60. Macro crosscurrents — including a mixed CPI print and the Iran ceasefire announcement — kept sentiment volatile throughout the week. (TKer by Sam Ro)

Why it matters: A 3.6% weekly bounce is encouraging but the index is not yet back in positive territory for the year, meaning one more geopolitical shock — say, a Hormuz ceasefire collapse — could quickly flip the narrative back to correction territory.

 
🔬 Science & Technology
A Humanoid Robot Just Moved Into a Real Home — Not a Lab, an Actual House

Chinese robotics firm UniX AI announced that its third-generation humanoid robot, Panther, has completed what the company calls the first-ever real-home deployment of a mass-produced humanoid, operating autonomously inside a private residence in Suzhou, Jiangsu. Unlike previous "home robot" demos that took place in controlled showroom environments, UniX says Panther is navigating an unmodified living space with real furniture, real clutter, and real humans. (Business Insider / GlobeNewswire)

Why it matters: If verified independently, this marks the moment the humanoid robot story graduates from trade-show theater to genuine consumer product — a milestone that will accelerate Western competitor timelines and regulatory conversations simultaneously.

Japan Bets Another $4 Billion on Domestic Chipmaker Rapidus

The Japanese government approved an additional $4 billion in funding for Rapidus, the domestic semiconductor startup it is backing in an ambitious bid to build leading-edge chip fabrication capability inside Japan for the first time in decades. The fresh capital injection brings total government support for Rapidus into the tens of billions of dollars as Tokyo races to reduce reliance on TSMC and Samsung for advanced logic chips. (TradingView / Reuters)

Why it matters: Semiconductor sovereignty is now a core national-security doctrine across the G7; Japan's sustained commitment to Rapidus will pressure the U.S. and EU to match the pace of industrial-policy spending or cede ground in the next generation of chip supply chains.
