Happy Friday — and what a week it has been. Iran–US diplomats are still talking, a New York jury just stuck a fork in Ticketmaster’s monopoly claims, and Wall Street is heading into the weekend in a surprisingly good mood. Grab your coffee; here’s everything you need to know.

🌎 Geopolitics & World Affairs
Iran–US Talks Edge Forward — But "Large Gaps" Still Loom

An Iranian official told reporters Thursday that negotiations with Washington are making "progress," but significant gaps remain on core issues including uranium enrichment rights. The White House has separately denied requesting a ceasefire extension, even as the Pentagon held a public briefing on the ongoing conflict. (Times of Israel, Reuters)

Why it matters: A failed deal doesn’t just restart the shooting — it risks pulling Saudi Arabia, Turkey, Egypt, and nuclear Pakistan into a formal "Sunni Axis" that reshapes Middle East security for a generation.

Pentagon Briefing: Israel Still Operating in Southern Lebanon

AP photojournalists captured Israeli army vehicles and bulldozers active in southern Lebanon as of Wednesday, even as ceasefire talks sputtered. The Pentagon’s Thursday briefing on the Iran war acknowledged the multi-front complexity, with Trump separately telling reporters he asked Xi Jinping not to supply Iran with weapons. (AP News, Reuters)

Why it matters: Continued IDF activity on the Lebanese front means any Iran nuclear deal must also resolve a separate, active ground situation — two clocks ticking at once.

 
💼 Business & Finance
Jury Declares Ticketmaster–Live Nation an Illegal Monopoly

A New York jury found Thursday that Ticketmaster and its parent company Live Nation illegally monopolized the U.S. live-event market, handing the Justice Department a landmark antitrust victory. The verdict caps a years-long battle that accused the company of locking out competitors from major concert venues. (Reuters)

Why it matters: A breakup order could restructure the entire live-entertainment industry, lowering fees for consumers and opening the door to genuine ticketing competition for the first time in decades.

S&P 500 Near Records as Wall Street Wades Deeper Into Earnings

Major indexes added to record highs Thursday, lifted by Iran de-escalation optimism and strong early earnings results. Looking ahead, Reuters notes the week’s next test is a deeper roster of S&P 500 reports, while Netflix results are drawing heavy attention from prediction markets. (Investopedia, Reuters, Seeking Alpha)

Why it matters: If earnings hold up alongside geopolitical cooling, the rally has fundamental legs — but New York Fed President Williams warned Thursday that the Iran war could still slow growth and aggravate inflation.

IMF & World Bank Gather Under a Cloud of Gloom

Economists converged this week for the annual IMF and World Bank spring meetings, with the mood decidedly downbeat amid war-driven commodity shocks, sticky inflation, and slowing global trade. The sessions come as the U.S. Treasury simultaneously ended the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau’s Washington office lease, signaling continued domestic deregulation. (Reuters)

Why it matters: A pessimistic IMF growth forecast — especially if it cuts emerging-market outlooks — could spook bond markets just as the equity rally looks fragile.

 
🔬 Science & Technology
AI Is Becoming a Scientist: Self-Driving Labs Are Here

A new Forbes analysis argues that AI has crossed a threshold from research tool to autonomous scientific actor, with "self-driving labs" now capable of designing experiments, running them robotically, and iterating on results without human intervention. Google DeepMind’s Demis Hassabis — who won the 2024 Nobel in chemistry for protein folding — calls AI "the best tool ever for accelerating discovery." (Forbes)

Why it matters: Compressing the drug-discovery and materials-science cycle from years to weeks isn’t a metaphor — it’s a structural shift in how humanity solves hard problems.

Forbes AI 50 Declares the Era of AI "Independence" — Raw Power Is Out, Real Results Are In

Forbes unveiled its 2026 AI 50 list Thursday, spotlighting a clear theme shift: the market no longer rewards companies for impressive demos but for measurable business outcomes. Many list members — including Harvey, Abridge, Cognition, and Cursor — were founded within the past three years yet already carry multibillion-dollar valuations. (Forbes)

Why it matters: The maturation signal is clear: enterprise buyers are closing their wallets on hype and opening them only for provable ROI, which will accelerate consolidation across the AI startup landscape.

School Dropout Bets Big: Factory AI Targets $1.5B Valuation

Factory, an AI startup building autonomous coding agents, is in talks to raise funding at a $1.5 billion valuation after an investor literally dared its co-founder to drop out and build full-time. The company enters a crowded field but is betting that truly agentic — not just copilot — coding automation is the next frontier. (Wall Street Journal)

Why it matters: If autonomous coding agents deliver, the software development workforce pyramid inverts — fewer junior engineers, more orchestrators — and it happens faster than most companies are planning for.

 
🇺 Society & Culture
Spain Opens a "Straightforward" Path to Migrant Regularization

An NGO praised Spain’s newly streamlined migrant regularization process Thursday as one of the clearest legal pathways in Europe for undocumented residents to gain status. The move comes as migration policy diverges sharply across the continent, with Hungary’s post-Orbán government signaling its own rethink on borders. (Reuters)

Why it matters: Spain’s model could become a template — or a political lightning rod — for the EU’s broader debate on labor shortages versus nativist pressure heading into the next European Parliament cycle.

Sudan’s Hunger Crisis in One Image: A Two-Month-Old Fighting Malnutrition

AP photographers documented two-month-old Saidal Altaher receiving malnutrition treatment at Port Sudan’s paediatric hospital stabilization center this week, one of dozens of striking images from a conflict that has largely fallen off front pages. The image is a reminder that Sudan’s civil war has created one of the world’s worst humanitarian disasters. (AP News)

Why it matters: With global attention locked on the Middle East and Ukraine, aid funding for Sudan continues to lag catastrophically — and famine conditions are spreading faster than relief can follow.
