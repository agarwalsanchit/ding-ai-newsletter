Good morning!

Inflation ticked up to its highest in nearly three years, GameStop is apparently trying to buy eBay (yes, really), and the Ukraine ceasefire is hanging by a thread — all before your second cup of coffee. Here’s what you need to know to sound smart today.

🌎 Geopolitics & World Affairs
Ukraine Ceasefire Teeters as Russia and Kyiv Trade Blame

The US-brokered ceasefire in Ukraine is approaching its deadline with neither side showing willingness to extend it, as Moscow and Kyiv each accuse the other of continued attacks. EU foreign ministers convened in Brussels Monday declaring "new momentum" on Ukraine, while separately imposing fresh sanctions on individuals linked to Russia’s mass abduction of Ukrainian children. (AP News, The Guardian)

Why it matters: A collapsed ceasefire would reset diplomatic progress to zero and ramp up pressure on European governments to commit fresh military and financial support to Kyiv ahead of a difficult winter.

Iran—US Impasse Deepens Ahead of Trump’s China Trip

Negotiations between Washington and Tehran remain deadlocked, with Trump publicly rejecting Iran’s latest peace proposal as "unacceptable" even as his China visit looms. The standoff is keeping the Strait of Hormuz under pressure, with Asian economies bracing for a second wave of energy shocks as the Iran war drags on and oil supply routes remain uncertain. (AP News, Investopedia)

Why it matters: Every day the Hormuz situation goes unresolved, energy-dependent economies in Japan, South Korea, and India absorb deeper cost shocks — and the window for a diplomatic reset before Trump’s China trip narrows further.

 
💼 Business & Finance
April CPI Hits 3.8% — Highest Since May 2023

Consumer prices rose 3.8% annually in April, the hottest inflation print in nearly three years, according to data released this morning and highlighted across CNBC’s pre-market coverage. Markets opened with caution as traders weigh whether the Federal Reserve has any runway left to consider rate cuts in 2026. (CNBC)

Why it matters: A sustained re-acceleration in inflation effectively takes near-term Fed rate cuts off the table, putting pressure on both borrowing costs and the record-high equity valuations markets have been celebrating for six straight weeks.

eBay Swats Away GameStop’s $56 Billion Takeover Bid

eBay’s board formally rejected a $56 billion acquisition offer from GameStop, a jaw-dropping move that underscores how flush the meme-stock-turned-investment-vehicle has become since CEO Ryan Cohen pivoted its strategy. eBay called the offer inadequate, leaving analysts debating whether Cohen will come back with a higher bid or set his sights elsewhere. (AP News)

Why it matters: If GameStop successfully acquires a major e-commerce platform, it would represent one of the most dramatic corporate reinventions in recent memory — and a massive test of whether Cohen’s cash-heavy balance sheet can translate into operational reality.

Spring Housing Market Is Officially a Bust

April home sales came in essentially flat, dashing hopes for the traditional spring rebound in real estate activity. Elevated mortgage rates and stubborn asking prices are keeping buyers on the sidelines, and with today’s hot CPI reading, relief from the Fed looks even more distant. (The Wall Street Journal)

Why it matters: A frozen housing market ripples into consumer spending on furniture, appliances, and services — and signals that high rates are doing real economic damage even as equity markets celebrate record highs.

 
🔬 Science & Technology
AI VC Funding in Q1 2026 Surpassed All of 2025 — in a Single Quarter

PitchBook’s Q1 2026 AI venture capital report reveals that $255.5 billion flowed into AI startups in just the first three months of the year, eclipsing the entire 2025 full-year total of $254.4 billion. Capital concentration is extreme, with a handful of large infrastructure and model-layer deals accounting for a disproportionate share. (PitchBook)

Why it matters: When a single quarter of AI investment outpaces a full prior year, it signals either a generational technology build-out or a bubble forming in slow motion — and the answer will define the next decade of enterprise tech.

Hackers Cut a Deal on Stolen Canvas Data as Finals Season Burns

A deal has been reached with the threat actors who stole data from the Canvas educational platform, with the hackers agreeing to delete the stolen information, according to new reporting out this morning. The breach, which disrupted access for millions of students during finals, had already rattled colleges scrambling to verify grade integrity and reschedule exams. (AP News)

Why it matters: "We paid the hackers to delete it" is not a cybersecurity strategy — and the Canvas breach is a loud warning about how dangerously under-secured the infrastructure underpinning higher education has become.

AI Startup Targets the $300B Unfilled Prescription Problem

Forus Health has built an AI system that intercepts a prescription the moment a physician writes it, instantly navigating insurance rules, formulary restrictions, and pharmacy eligibility to ensure it actually gets filled. The startup is targeting a gap that costs the US healthcare system hundreds of billions of dollars annually in abandoned or delayed medications. (Forbes)

Why it matters: Unfilled prescriptions are a leading cause of avoidable hospitalizations — an AI fix here could reduce costs and improve outcomes at the same time, which is rarer than it sounds in healthcare.
