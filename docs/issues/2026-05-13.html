🌎 Geopolitics & World Affairs
Trump and Xi Meet in Beijing as Trade, Taiwan, and Iran All Land on the Table

President Trump arrived in Beijing this week for a high-stakes summit with President Xi Jinping, with the agenda packed: ongoing trade tensions, the future of Taiwan, and the ripple effects of the U.S.—Iran war all demanding attention. The summit comes at a moment of unusual geopolitical convergence, with energy markets rattled and both economies feeling tariff pain. Reports have also surfaced of a potential secret Chinese weapons arrangement with Iran, adding an explosive subtext to the talks. (AP News, Khabarhub)

Why it matters: The outcome of this summit could determine whether the Iran conflict escalates further, whether tariffs ease, and whether global energy markets get any relief — all at once.

Rickshaw Bomb Kills 9 in Pakistan Bazaar as Regional Violence Grinds On

A bomb strapped to a rickshaw detonated in a crowded bazaar in Pakistan’s Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province on Tuesday, killing 9 people and wounding more than two dozen others. The attack in Lakki Marwat adds to a sustained pattern of militant violence in the region. (AP News)

Why it matters: With regional security already strained by the broader Iran conflict and an ongoing Pakistan—India standoff, continued domestic terrorism in Pakistan risks further destabilizing an already fragile nuclear-armed state.

 
💼 Business & Finance
Producer Prices Spike 6%, Heaping More Pain on a 3.8% CPI Already Stung by Iran War Energy Costs

Fresh data released Wednesday showed U.S. producer prices jumped 6% — a sharp acceleration that signals businesses will face mounting pressure to pass costs on to consumers. This follows Tuesday’s April CPI reading of 3.8%, the highest since May 2023, with energy prices driven sharply higher by the ongoing U.S.—Iran conflict. (AP News)

Why it matters: A 6% PPI print with CPI already at 3.8% dramatically narrows the Federal Reserve’s room to cut rates, and virtually guarantees further price increases at the shelf level in the coming months.

eBay Sends GameStop’s $56 Billion Takeover Bid Back to the Bargain Bin

eBay’s board has formally rejected a $56 billion takeover offer from GameStop, led by CEO Ryan Cohen, calling the bid inadequate. The unsolicited offer had raised eyebrows on Wall Street given the stark contrast between the two companies’ recent trajectories. (AP News)

Why it matters: The rebuff leaves Cohen’s ambitious consolidation strategy in limbo and raises serious questions about what GameStop intends to do with its massive cash reserves — and whether a sweetened bid could follow.

 
🔮 Science & Technology
AI VC Funding in Q1 2026 Alone Eclipsed Every Dollar Invested in All of 2025

New data from PitchBook reveals that total AI venture funding reached $255.5 billion in just the first quarter of 2026, surpassing the full-year 2025 figure of $254.4 billion. Three deals alone accounted for 67% of the capital deployed, reflecting the extreme concentration of investment around frontier model companies. Anthropic recently reported a run-rate revenue exceeding $30 billion, while OpenAI crossed $25 billion in annualized revenue. (PitchBook)

Why it matters: The sheer scale of capital now flowing into AI — outpacing traditional VC capacity — signals that sovereign funds and strategic investors are reshaping how the next generation of foundational technology gets built and controlled.

Hackers Strike a Deal to Delete Stolen Canvas Data as Students Exhale — Barely

A deal has been reached with the hackers behind the cyberattack on Canvas, the widely used educational platform, under which the stolen data will be deleted rather than published or sold. The breach had locked millions of students out of their coursework, grades, and exams during peak finals season. (AP News, MediaPost)

Why it matters: Even if the data is deleted, the attack exposed catastrophic single-point-of-failure risk in edtech infrastructure — and demonstrated that ransomware actors are increasingly targeting academic calendars for maximum leverage.
