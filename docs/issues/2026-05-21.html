🧠 AI & Technology
OpenAI's AI Solves an 80-Year-Old Math Problem — By Itself

OpenAI announced that one of its general-purpose reasoning models autonomously solved a famous geometry problem that had stumped mathematicians for eight decades — without being prompted to do so. Separately, Anthropic's explosive growth has the company on track for revenues that would have seemed fantastical just two years ago. (Axios)

Why it matters: Original mathematical discovery by AI isn't a parlor trick — it signals a potential inflection point where AI stops retrieving known answers and starts generating genuinely new knowledge, with downstream consequences for science, engineering, and medicine.

White House AI Executive Order Lands — Watch This Space

The White House's long-anticipated AI executive order was expected to drop as soon as Wednesday, with new details emerging about its scope — which reportedly includes provisions touching AI governance, model oversight, and deployment standards. The order arrives as Google's I/O showcase this week unveiled its AI agent “Spark” and a universal shopping cart that stitches together multiple merchant sites. (The Washington Post, Business Insider)

Why it matters: An executive order sets the regulatory tone for the entire U.S. AI industry ahead of a pivotal midterm election cycle in which AI is already becoming a campaign-finance force.

 
🌎 Geopolitics & World Affairs
Ukraine's Drones Strike Deep — Again — as Long-Range Campaign Intensifies

Ukraine confirmed its drones hit another oil refinery deep inside Russian territory on Thursday, the latest in an escalating long-range strike campaign that has now targeted Russian logistics, military equipment, and energy infrastructure throughout early 2026. A Washington-based think tank assessed that the intensified campaign has “degraded Russian forces' ability to conduct offensive operations” and likely supported recent Ukrainian battlefield advances. (AP News)

Why it matters: Striking refineries deep inside Russia directly degrades the fuel supply chain sustaining Russian frontline operations, and marks a qualitative escalation in Ukraine's ability to impose strategic costs far from the front lines.

EU Ties $8 Billion in Ukraine Aid to Unpopular Tax Reforms

The European Commission confirmed that Ukraine must improve revenue collection and spending efficiency to unlock more than $8 billion in loan disbursements — conditions Kyiv's government has characterized as politically painful. The conditionality comes as Ukraine's wartime budget remains heavily dependent on Western financial support. (The Kyiv Independent)

Why it matters: Structural fiscal reforms demanded mid-war force Kyiv to balance battlefield needs against domestic political capital, and could slow aid disbursements at a critical juncture.

 
📈 Markets & Finance
Nvidia Posts a Blowout — Wall Street Shrugs, Then Slips

Nvidia delivered yet another record-breaking earnings report, but U.S. stocks slipped Thursday as oil prices reversed higher and investors appeared to have already priced in the good news. The Dow had surged nearly 700 points Wednesday ahead of the print, meaning Thursday's “sell the news” reaction was almost entirely mechanical. (The Sun Chronicle, Investopedia)

Why it matters: When a company posting historic profits gets a yawn, it signals either peak AI euphoria or a market hunting for its next catalyst — both of which have significant implications for tech sector valuations through summer.

“Overriding Issue for Bond Markets Is Inflation” — Not Politics

Macro analysts told Reuters Thursday that despite a packed geopolitical calendar — Xi–Putin talks in Beijing, Iran tensions, and U.S. fiscal debates — the dominant driver for global bond markets remains the inflation trajectory, not political noise. A global bond selloff that rattled markets earlier this week eased as Asian shares surged on falling oil and the AI rally resumed. (Reuters, AP News)

Why it matters: If inflation re-accelerates — particularly as oil prices whipsaw — central banks lose the room to cut, and the equity rally built on rate-cut expectations becomes vulnerable across all asset classes.

 
🌎 Society & Culture
San Diego Mosque Attack: Teens Cited Prior Atrocities as Inspiration

Investigators revealed Thursday that the teenagers behind the attack on the Islamic Center of San Diego explicitly cited prior mass-casualty atrocities — including the Christchurch massacre — as inspiration, fitting a pattern of ideological “copycat” radicalization seen in other recent attacks. The disclosure came as vigils continued outside the center and Democratic Senate candidate Abdul El-Sayed convened an interfaith press conference in Michigan. (AP News)

Why it matters: The explicit citation of prior atrocities underscores how online extremist content continues to serve as a radicalization pipeline, keeping this attack squarely in the national debate over hate crime prevention and domestic terrorism.

Australia Fines X $465,000 for Online Safety Breach After Three-Year Battle

An Australian judge handed X (formerly Twitter) a $465,000 fine Thursday following a three-year legal battle over violations of the country's online safety laws — a ruling that represents one of the first successful government-imposed financial penalties on the platform under Elon Musk's ownership. (AP News)

Why it matters: Modest as the fine is, the precedent matters: it establishes that national courts can successfully impose safety-related penalties on X, potentially emboldening regulators in the EU and beyond to pursue similar actions.
