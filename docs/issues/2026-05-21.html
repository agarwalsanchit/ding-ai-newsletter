Ukraine’s drones are reaching deeper into Russia than ever, a White House AI executive order is expected any moment, and Australia just handed Elon Musk’s X a bill it probably won’t enjoy paying. It’s a busy Thursday — let’s get into it.

🌎 Geopolitics & World Affairs
Ukraine Drones Strike Deep Inside Russia as Long-Range Campaign Intensifies

Ukraine says its drones struck another oil refinery deep inside Russian territory on Thursday, marking a fresh escalation in its long-range strike campaign. The attack follows a pattern of increasingly ambitious drone operations targeting Russian energy infrastructure, even as Russian air attacks on Ukrainian cities continued, including strikes on the Dnipropetrovsk region. (AP News)

Why it matters: Sustained hits on Russian refineries erode Moscow’s domestic fuel supply and export revenue — a slow economic bleed that complements battlefield pressure and raises the stakes for any ceasefire diplomacy.

BRICS Summit in New Delhi Draws Global Attention as Xi and Putin Expected to Attend

The 2026 BRICS Summit in New Delhi is gaining outsized geopolitical significance, with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin both expected to attend amid the ongoing Iran conflict and rising US-China tensions. The gathering comes as the world faces a "dangerous phase" of diplomacy, with analysts warning that the pool of credible international mediators is shrinking fast. (Sunday Guardian Live, Daily Times)

Why it matters: A Xi-Putin meeting on Indian soil would signal a deepening of the Russia-China axis at a moment when Western alliances are already strained — and could reshape the diplomatic landscape around both the Ukraine war and the Iran standoff.

Hormuz, Blockades, and Energy Leverage: The Geopolitical Flashpoints Defining May

Control of the Strait of Hormuz has emerged as the central strategic battleground in the ongoing US-Iran confrontation, with analysts warning that whoever commands the strait effectively controls a chokepoint for a fifth of the world’s oil supply. The World Economic Forum’s May geopolitical roundup also highlights how naval blockades are becoming a normalized diplomatic tool and energy supply chains are being weaponized as political levers. (Modern Diplomacy, World Economic Forum)

Why it matters: Any disruption at Hormuz would send oil prices spiking globally within hours — a risk that is now a live scenario, not a hypothetical, with direct consequences for inflation, markets, and allied military posture.

 
💼 Business & Finance
Nvidia Posts Another Blowout — Markets Respond With a Shrug

Nvidia delivered yet another blockbuster earnings report, but markets gave the results a notably muted reception on Thursday, with US stocks slipping as oil prices climbed and investor enthusiasm failed to ignite. The day prior had seen the Dow surge nearly 700 points in anticipation, suggesting traders had already priced in the beat. (The Sun Chronicle, Investopedia)

Why it matters: When a company that has defined the AI bull run can’t move markets on a record quarter, it signals that the easy gains from AI enthusiasm may be behind us — and oil volatility is reasserting itself as the dominant macro driver.

Australia Fines X $465,000 for Online Safety Breach After Three-Year Battle

An Australian judge has fined Elon Musk’s X (formerly Twitter) AUD $465,000 following a three-year court battle over violations of the country’s online safety laws. The ruling caps a prolonged regulatory confrontation between X and Australia’s eSafety Commissioner that became a flashpoint in global debates about platform accountability. (AP News)

Why it matters: While the fine is modest relative to X’s scale, the precedent emboldens regulators in other jurisdictions and adds to a growing pile of legal and reputational pressure on the platform at a critical moment for its ad revenue recovery.

 
🔬 Science & Technology
White House AI Executive Order Expected Imminently — Here’s What to Watch For

The Trump White House’s highly anticipated AI executive order is expected to drop as soon as Thursday, according to a scoop from The Washington Post’s AI & Tech Brief. The order arrives in the same week Google unveiled a sweeping suite of AI products at I/O 2026 — including a universal shopping cart — and as Google CEO Sundar Pichai revealed that AI Overviews now reaches over 2.5 billion monthly users. (Washington Post, CNBC)

Why it matters: A presidential executive order sets the regulatory and procurement tone for AI across the entire federal government and signals to the private sector which direction the guardrails — or lack thereof — will point for the next several years.

AI Is Becoming a Political Finance Machine Ahead of the Midterms

AI is shaping up as a major force in campaign finance ahead of the 2026 midterms, with Scale AI launching a seven-figure ad campaign in New York and San Francisco branded "the humans stay." Separately, AI watchdog group the Midas Project has alleged industry dirty tactics in the political arena, prompting OpenAI’s Chief Global Affairs Officer Chris Lehane to publicly distance the company from the claims. (Washington Post)

Why it matters: AI companies pouring money into political messaging while simultaneously lobbying against regulation creates a conflict-of-interest dynamic that voters, lawmakers, and regulators are only beginning to scrutinize.
