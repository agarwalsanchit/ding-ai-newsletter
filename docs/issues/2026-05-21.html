🚨 Top News
Anthropic Co-Founder Predicts AI Will Win a Nobel Prize Within a Year

Jack Clark, co-founder of Anthropic, delivered a lecture at Oxford University on Wednesday in which he predicted that AI will contribute to a Nobel prize-winning scientific discovery within a year. Clark outlined a spectrum of anticipated changes, from his most conservative forecast — 'vast swathes of the economy and society will go through profound changes' — to more radical possibilities including a machine economy decoupling from the human economy, robots acquiring general intelligence, science progressing autonomously, and entirely new categories of scientific equipment being invented by AI. Clark acknowledged that some of these predictions sounded 'crazy.' (The Guardian)

Why it matters: Predictions from senior figures at frontier AI labs carry market and policy weight, influencing research funding, regulatory debates, and public expectations about the pace of AI-driven disruption in science and the economy. A Nobel-caliber AI discovery within 12 months, if it materialises, would fundamentally reframe debates about human roles in scientific research.

 
🌍 Geopolitics & World Affairs
Xi and Putin Meet in Beijing, Declare Ties at Record High

Xi Jinping hosted Vladimir Putin in Beijing on May 20, 2026, with both leaders publicly praising the depth of China-Russia relations. The summit included a formal welcome ceremony and statements underscoring the closeness of the two countries' partnership. The meeting comes shortly after Trump held talks with Xi on May 15, during which Boeing sales, Taiwan, and Iranian oil were discussed. The Beijing summit signals continued strategic alignment between Moscow and Beijing even as both navigate separate diplomatic channels with Washington. (Reuters)

Why it matters: The Xi-Putin summit deepens the China-Russia strategic axis at a moment when Xi is simultaneously cultivating warmer ties with Washington, giving Beijing unusual leverage in global geopolitics and complicating U.S. efforts to isolate Moscow.

South Africa Nominates Five Universities for Presidential PhD Programme Amid Funding Shortfall

South Africa's National Research Foundation has selected five universities as prospective key implementers of the country's Presidential PhD Programme, a flagship initiative backed at the highest political level. However, the programme continues to face a significant funding gap compared to commitments made at its launch. The weekly Africa research roundup also notes a US ban on travellers linked to the Ebola outbreak, the evacuation of polar researchers, a delayed NSFAS (student financial aid) hearing, and the launch of a national science month — signalling a busy but constrained week for African research institutions. (Research Professional News)

Why it matters: The funding gap in South Africa's Presidential PhD Programme risks undermining a high-profile commitment to building postgraduate research capacity on the continent — a gap that, if not closed, could set back efforts to expand Africa's scientific talent pipeline at a critical moment.
