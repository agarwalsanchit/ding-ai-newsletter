🏆 Sports & Entertainment
PSG Beats Arsenal 4–3 on Penalties to Win the Champions League

Paris Saint-Germain defeated Arsenal in a pulsating Champions League final decided by a penalty shootout, ending 4–3 after the sides could not be separated in normal and extra time. Fans of both clubs packed the stadium with rival anthems before kickoff in what AP News described as a clash of two of Europe’s most passionate fanbases. It marks PSG’s first Champions League title and a heartbreaking near-miss for an Arsenal side that also claimed the Premier League crown this season.

Why it matters: PSG’s triumph rewrites French football history and raises immediate questions about Arsenal’s ability to convert domestic dominance into European glory — a debate that will dominate the summer transfer window.

Claude Lemieux’s Family Donates His Brain to BU’s CTE Research Center

The family of former Montreal Canadiens enforcer Claude Lemieux has announced they will donate his brain to Boston University’s CTE Center, one of the world’s leading chronic traumatic encephalopathy research institutions. Lemieux, widely remembered for his physical, combative style across multiple Stanley Cup–winning teams, had been seen publicly as recently as Game 3 of the NHL Eastern Conference final, carrying the torch into the arena in Montreal. AP News reported the announcement Sunday.

Why it matters: A high-profile donation from an NHL icon adds fresh urgency to the league’s ongoing reckoning with head trauma, and will provide researchers with data that could directly influence future player safety rules and equipment standards.

 
🌎 Geopolitics & World Affairs
Allies Warn at Shangri-La: Western Division Is a Gift to Adversaries

At the Shangri-La Dialogue security forum in Singapore on Sunday, senior defense officials from Japan, Australia, and the Philippines warned that fractures within Western alliances are actively undermining deterrence against China, Russia, and other emerging threats. Japan’s Defense Minister Shinjiro Koizumi and Philippine Defense Secretary Gilberto Teodoro both called for renewed unity in their addresses, while Australia’s Richard Marles echoed similar concerns on Saturday. The warnings follow U.S. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth’s notably softened rhetoric on China, which he offered at the same forum a day earlier. AP News has full coverage of both sessions.

Why it matters: When core U.S. treaty allies feel compelled to publicly lecture on alliance cohesion, it signals real anxiety about American reliability — and creates openings that Beijing and Moscow are well-positioned to exploit.

Belarus Emerges as Potential Launchpad for a New Russian Offensive Into Ukraine

Military analysts and Western officials are raising alarms that Belarus could be used as a staging ground for a renewed Russian push into northern Ukraine, according to a report from AP News. The concern comes amid renewed visible closeness between Vladimir Putin and Belarusian President Alexander Lukashenko, who appeared together at Moscow’s Victory Day commemorations on May 9. Simultaneously, Ukraine continues to strike Russian oil infrastructure, keeping economic pressure on Moscow even as Kyiv braces for intensified bombardment.

Why it matters: A northern front opened via Belarus would dramatically stretch Ukrainian defensive lines, potentially forcing Kyiv to redeploy forces away from the already contested east — the worst-case scenario NATO planners have been gaming out for months.

 
📈 Business & Finance
Wall Street Closes Out a Blistering May with Ninth Straight Weekly Gain

U.S. stock indexes closed higher on Friday, May 29, capping a strong month that saw the S&P 500 notch its ninth consecutive weekly advance — a streak that underscores how resilient investor sentiment has been despite ongoing geopolitical tensions. Dell Technologies was among the standout performers, surging on strong earnings, while optimism around a potential Iran ceasefire deal added a tailwind to oil markets and broader risk appetite. Investopedia, the Wall Street Journal, and Investor’s Business Daily all highlighted the milestone close.

Why it matters: Nine straight weeks of gains is a rare display of momentum — but it also raises the stakes for the upcoming jobs report, which could either validate the rally or hand bears their first real ammunition since early spring.

 
🔬 Science & Technology
Royal Observatory Warns AI Could Make Humans Less Intelligent

The Royal Observatory has weighed in on the AI dependence debate, with its director Paddy Rodgers cautioning that over-reliance on artificial intelligence tools risks eroding the very cognitive capabilities that drove centuries of human discovery. Drawing on the Observatory’s own history of painstaking human-led scientific inquiry, Rodgers argued that “dependence” on AI outputs — rather than using them as a supplement — poses a long-term threat to independent human reasoning. The warning was reported by BBC News.

Why it matters: As agentic AI tools embed deeper into education and professional workflows, the question of cognitive atrophy is moving from philosophical thought experiment to a genuine policy challenge for schools and employers.

UK and France Sign Landmark Sci-Tech Pact on Disease and Women’s Health

Britain and France have formalized a bilateral science and technology agreement that will see researchers from both nations collaborate on cures for infectious diseases and innovations in women’s health, according to an announcement from the UK government on Friday. The deal is framed as a post-Brexit normalisation of scientific ties and is expected to benefit millions of patients across both countries. Gov.uk published the full details of the agreement.

Why it matters: Pooling research capacity between two of Europe’s largest science economies could accelerate clinical timelines significantly — and signals that London and Paris are choosing pragmatic cooperation over post-Brexit posturing.
