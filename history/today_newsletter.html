🌎 Geopolitics & World Affairs
US and Iran Head Back to the Table — Ceasefire Clock Is Ticking

US and Iranian delegations are preparing for another round of negotiations as the Iran–Israel–Lebanon ceasefire enters a fragile phase, with Secretary Hegseth warning Tehran to "choose wisely." Trump has publicly stated the war "should be ending pretty soon," and the Israel–Lebanon ceasefire that began last week is holding — for now. (ABC News via Modern Ghana, Reuters)

Why it matters: A durable deal would reshape energy markets, regional security architecture, and the political fortunes of everyone from Tehran to Tel Aviv — but the window is narrow and both sides are still trading contradictory signals.

Hormuz Whiplash Continues: Iran Says "Strict Control," Markets Brace

Just 24 hours after Iran’s foreign minister told the world the Strait of Hormuz was "completely open," sending oil prices diving and the Dow up 900 points on Friday, Iran reversed course Saturday — declaring the strait is "under strict control" of Iranian forces. Shipowners remain cautious about transiting the waterway even during the ceasefire. (Investor’s Business Daily, CNBC)

Why it matters: About 20% of global oil supply flows through Hormuz; Iran’s flip-flopping in under a day is a reminder that market euphoria over Middle East de-escalation can evaporate just as fast as it arrives.

 
📈 Business & Finance
Records on Friday, Uncertainty on Sunday — Tesla Earnings Loom Large

The S&P 500 and Nasdaq notched record closes on Friday, buoyed by Middle East peace optimism and the Iran foreign minister’s (short-lived) Hormuz reassurance. But with Iran reversing its stance over the weekend and Tesla headlining a packed earnings calendar this week, futures traders are staring down meaningful downside risk when markets open Sunday evening. (Investor’s Business Daily, CNBC)

Why it matters: Record highs built on geopolitical sentiment — rather than earnings fundamentals — are uniquely fragile; this week’s results will tell us whether the rally has legs or is running on fumes.

Oil Stocks Whipsawed as Energy Sector Tries to Find Its Footing

Chevron and ExxonMobil slumped to multi-month lows on Friday before partially recovering as oil prices swung wildly on competing Iran signals. The energy sector was the clear loser in a day when almost every other corner of the market celebrated. (Investor’s Business Daily)

Why it matters: Oil majors are a bellwether for global growth expectations — and their inability to hold a bottom even on a record market day signals that energy investors simply do not trust the ceasefire narrative yet.

 
🔬 Science & Technology
A Robot Just Beat Every Human in a Half-Marathon — And It Wasn’t Even Close

A bipedal humanoid robot developed by smartphone maker Honor blazed through Beijing’s 13.1-mile half-marathon course in just 50 minutes — faster than the human world record for the distance. Several other humanoid robots also finished ahead of the fastest human runners in the field. (Reuters, NBC News)

Why it matters: Bipedal locomotion has long been robotics’ hardest problem; a sub-50-minute half-marathon signals that humanoid endurance, stability, and real-world navigation have crossed a threshold that will accelerate deployment in warehouses, disaster response, and beyond.

Americans Are Firing Dr. Google and Hiring AI Instead

New polling data shows a significant share of Americans now turn to AI chatbots to research symptoms before — or instead of — calling a doctor, marking a decisive shift away from traditional search. The trend is accelerating as AI tools become faster, more conversational, and increasingly accurate on medical queries. (Newser)

Why it matters: AI health triage at scale could reduce unnecessary ER visits and democratize basic medical guidance — but it also raises urgent questions about liability, misinformation, and what happens when the model is confidently wrong.

Perovskite Solar Hits 26.61% Certified Efficiency — A New Record

Researchers have achieved a certified 26.61% power-conversion efficiency in perovskite solar cells using a cesium-doping strategy, pushing the technology past another milestone on the road to commercial viability. The figure edges closer to the theoretical limits of silicon and positions perovskites as a serious rival to the dominant solar technology. (Tech Xplore)

Why it matters: Every efficiency percentage point gained translates directly into cheaper electricity per panel — and perovskites can be manufactured at a fraction of silicon’s cost, potentially unlocking solar for markets priced out today.

 
🏅 Sports
Ben Shelton Wins Munich Open, Adds Clay Credibility Before Roland Garros

American Ben Shelton defeated Italy’s Flavio Cobolli 6–2, 7–5 in the Munich Open final on Sunday, claiming his first ATP clay-court title and arriving at the European clay season in genuine form. The victory was commanding from start to finish, with Shelton converting on his big serve even on the slower surface. (AP News)

Why it matters: Clay has historically been Shelton’s weakest surface, and a title here reframes him as a genuine Roland Garros contender — not just a hardcourt specialist — heading into the season’s most grueling major.
