Ceasefire Holds — Barely — as Oil Prices Jump 3% on Hormuz Doubts

The two-week US—Iran ceasefire tied to reopening the Strait of Hormuz is being stress-tested almost immediately, with oil prices surging roughly 3% on Thursday as traders priced in the risk it falls apart. China and Russia vetoed a UN Security Council resolution aimed at protecting Hormuz shipping, removing any multilateral safety net. Trump hailed the original deal as "a big day for World Peace!" on social media, though markets appear unconvinced. (Reuters, CBS News, Modern Ghana / ABC News)

Why it matters: A collapse of the ceasefire would choke roughly 20% of global oil supply through the Strait of Hormuz, sending energy prices sharply higher and amplifying inflation pressures worldwide just as economies are still digesting tariff shocks.

UK and Norway Execute Major Anti-Submarine Operation in the North Atlantic

British and Norwegian forces led a coordinated military operation to deter Russian submarine activity in the North Atlantic, with details released Thursday. The operation reflects growing NATO concern over Russian undersea movements at a moment when alliance attention is already stretched across multiple theaters. (AP News)

Why it matters: Russian submarine operations in the North Atlantic threaten undersea communications cables and NATO resupply lanes — a quiet but critical front that rarely makes headlines until something goes wrong.

North Korea Wraps Three-Day Missile Blitz Featuring Cluster Warhead and "Electromagnetic Weapon"

Pyongyang announced Thursday the conclusion of a three-day missile test campaign that included a cluster-warhead variant of the Hwasong-11 Ka ballistic missile, a new "electromagnetic weapon system," and a short-range surface-to-air missile. South Korean and Japanese military authorities tracked the multiple launches. (Aviation Week)

Why it matters: Cluster-warhead and electromagnetic weapons signal North Korea is actively diversifying its ability to saturate missile defenses and disable electronics — a direct challenge to US and allied deterrence architecture in the Pacific.

Anthropic's Claude Mythos Uncovers Thousands of Zero-Day Vulnerabilities — With Project Glasswing

Anthropic announced a cybersecurity initiative called Project Glasswing, deploying a preview version of its Claude Mythos model that has already identified thousands of previously unknown zero-day security flaws across major software systems. Why it matters: AI-powered vulnerability discovery at this scale is a double-edged sword — it can help defenders patch systems faster, but the same capability in adversarial hands could enable unprecedented cyberattack campaigns before patches are deployed.

Anthropic is partnering with Google and Broadcom to develop custom silicon as part of a broader hardware strategy estimated to involve up to $50 billion in infrastructure spending, with facilities planned in New York and Texas.
