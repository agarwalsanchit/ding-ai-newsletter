🔬 Science & Technology
Trump Administration to Ask U.S. AI Firms to Voluntarily Submit Models for Cybersecurity Testing

The Trump administration announced on June 3, 2026, that it will ask U.S. AI companies to voluntarily submit their models for cybersecurity testing. The initiative is framed as a response to growing fears about the risks of increasingly powerful AI systems. Because the submission is voluntary rather than mandatory, companies retain the choice of whether to participate. The move follows a broader pattern of the administration engaging with AI governance through executive action, including a recent executive order requiring national security vetting of top AI models, signaling an evolving federal approach to managing AI risks without imposing hard regulatory mandates. (IT News)

Why it matters: A voluntary cybersecurity testing regime for AI models sets a policy precedent but its effectiveness hinges on industry uptake — if major AI developers opt out, the program may offer limited protection against the security vulnerabilities it aims to identify.

Mathematicians Urge Caution, Call AI Capabilities 'Overhyped'

A group of mathematicians published a statement on June 2, 2026, urging the public and their peers not to believe the hype surrounding current AI capabilities. The researchers called on fellow mathematicians to critically evaluate the ethical consequences of their work with AI and to withdraw from projects they deem harmful. The statement reflects growing concern within the academic mathematics community about inflated claims being made by AI developers and advocates, and the potential societal risks of deploying systems whose capabilities are widely misunderstood or misrepresented. (Phys.org)

Why it matters: When mathematicians — a community central to AI's theoretical foundations — publicly warn against capability hype and urge ethical withdrawal, it signals a credibility crisis that could influence research funding, academic collaboration, and public trust in AI benchmarks.

The Skift Data and AI Summit 2026 convened in New York City on June 3, 2026, gathering travel industry executives, data specialists, and technology innovators. New Zealand Town of Oamaru Hosts Steampunk Festival, Becomes Global Hub for Victorian Sci-Fi Culture

Oamaru, a small town on New Zealand's South Island, has carved out an international identity as a steampunk destination, hosting a festival that celebrates the blending of Victorian-era style with science fiction and fantasy. The event draws participants in elaborate costumes featuring gears, goggles, corsets, and imagined technologies from an alternate steam-powered age. Steampunk has grown from a literary subgenre into a global subculture with dedicated festivals, art markets, and communities. Oamaru's existing Victorian-era architecture — a legacy of its 19th-century gold rush prosperity — provides an authentic backdrop that has made it a natural fit for the aesthetic. (AP News)

Why it matters: Oamaru's steampunk festival illustrates how niche subcultures can transform small communities into internationally recognized cultural destinations, with implications for cultural tourism and community identity-building.
