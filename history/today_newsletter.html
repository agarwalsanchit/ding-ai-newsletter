🔬 Science & Technology
DeepMind's Demis Hassabis Says AI Could Make a Postgrad as Productive as an Entire Lab

Demis Hassabis, co-founder of Google DeepMind and Nobel laureate, told the 2026 Nobel Prize Dialogue London on May 26 that AI has the potential to make a single postgraduate researcher as productive as an entire laboratory. He argued AI will liberate PhD students and postdoctoral researchers from lower-level tasks, enabling them to focus on higher-order scientific thinking. The comments reflect growing optimism — and debate — in the research community about how AI will restructure scientific labor and discovery processes. (Research Professional News)

Why it matters: If Hassabis's prediction proves accurate, AI could fundamentally restructure academic research institutions, compress the timeline for scientific breakthroughs, and shift what skills are valued in training the next generation of scientists.

Enterprise Technology Association Launches AIReadyAmerica.org to Close AI Skills Gap

The Enterprise Technology Association (ETA) launched AIReadyAmerica.org on May 27, 2026, at the US AI Congress in Washington. The initiative aims to accelerate AI workforce development and economic growth nationwide. Leaders from across the country and federal government convened at the event, with two dominant themes emerging: widening skills gaps and increasing economic risks tied to AI adoption. The platform is designed to coordinate efforts to prepare American workers for an AI-driven economy. (PR Newswire)

Why it matters: As AI reshapes job requirements across sectors, coordinated national workforce initiatives like AIReadyAmerica.org could determine whether large segments of the U.S. labor force are included in or left behind by the AI economy. Without such efforts, skills gaps risk deepening economic inequality.

SecurityWeek announced it will host the 2026 AI Risk Summit on August 11–12 at the Ritz-Carlton in Half Moon Bay, California.
