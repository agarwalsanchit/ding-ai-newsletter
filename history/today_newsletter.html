The United Nations is moving to vote on a scaled-back resolution calling for the Strait of Hormuz to be reopened, with Russia and China seen as the pivotal swing votes that could kill or shape any consensus. The measure has been weakened considerably from earlier drafts in a bid to secure broader support. (AP News)

Why it matters: A vetoed or toothless resolution would leave diplomatic cover thin and increase the odds that the US acts unilaterally — a scenario markets and allies are watching with dread.

Iran, Hezbollah & Houthis Coordinate Simultaneous Strikes on Israel

Why it matters: A tri-front coordinated strike signals deliberate escalation strategy, raising the stakes for Israel's response calculus and the broader US-Iran confrontation.

Artemis II Crew Sends Back Stunning "Earthset" Photo from Lunar Orbit

The four-person Artemis II crew — Commander Reid Wiseman, Pilot Victor Glover, and Mission Specialists Christina Koch and Jeremy Hansen — captured a striking Earthset image from lunar orbit, evoking the iconic Apollo 8 photographs of 1968. The crew also photographed a solar eclipse as seen from the Moon on April 6. (AP News, NASA)

Why it matters: These images are more than beautiful — they mark the first time humans have photographed Earth from lunar distance in over 50 years, cementing Artemis II's place in spaceflight history before it even returns home.

School Districts Move to Bar Students Without Legal Status

A growing number of school districts across the United States are voting on measures to exclude students without legal immigration status, a move that would directly challenge a decades-old Supreme Court precedent established in Plyler v. Why it matters: If any district successfully enforces such an exclusion, it will trigger immediate legal battles that could reach the Supreme Court and redefine the constitutional right to public education for millions of children.
